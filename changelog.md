# Changelog

## [3.4.0]

### New Content
- **Hybrid Pressure Model:** Until now every hull block held down to its own max depth, whatever the shape of the ship, so a huge flat wall of iron held as deep as a small round cabin made of the same blocks. A new server option, `pressureModel`, picks between the two models: **Classic** keeps the old behaviour, **Hybrid** (the new default) scales each block's depth by the shape of the hull around it. Wide flat walls hold less, while small panels, thick walls, ribs and rounded hulls hold more, from half to twice the block's depth (`shapeFactorMin` / `shapeFactorMax`). A single-thickness wall `shapeReferenceSpan` blocks wide (7 by default) keeps exactly its block's depth. Every outer face of the hull is checked, sides included, not only the floor and the roof. When the hull gives way, the cracks now land on the most overloaded blocks first instead of a random weak block. Ships with more than `shapeMaxBlocks` solid blocks skip the analysis and use the Classic depths. `/submarine info` shows the model in use and the weakest block of the hull.
- **Girder Beams:** Metal Girders now hold a hull together under pressure.
  - **What counts:** a straight line of Metal Girders (or Encased Girders) that touches a solid hull block at both ends. It can stand upright between the floor and the ceiling, or lie flat between two walls, along either side of the ship. A beam with a loose end, or one that ends on air, glass or any block water goes through, does nothing.
  - **Stronger hull:** around both ends of a beam, the hull holds up to 60% deeper right at the contact, and the bonus fades out over 4 blocks. Close beams do not stack: each block keeps the best one.
  - **The beam takes the load:** when the water gets deep enough to crack a hull block that a beam is holding, the hull does not crack. The beam carries the load instead and starts to give way: its middle girder tears loose and shakes in place for 4 seconds, harder and harder, while it creaks louder and louder, smoke and metal bits pour out of it and the whole ship shudders. The hull around the beam holds while it shakes.
  - **Blowing out:** after 4 seconds the girder is shot out of the beam as a loose, bouncy block. Before it goes, the ship looks around it for open room so the girder flies at a wall and bounces around the hull instead of getting stuck between the ends of the beam. Every time it slams into an outer wall, the wall it hits cracks. Inside a dry, sealed hull the loose girder then drops to the floor instead of floating in the water outside the ship.
  - **Damaged area:** the beam no longer reaches both ends, so it stops holding anything, and the area around its two contacts is left damaged: the hull there now holds 40% less deep and starts cracking much sooner. Rebuilding a beam between the same two points repairs the area.
  - **Pressure Goggles:** the areas a beam reinforces show as cooler.
- **Pressure Goggles:** Engineer's Goggles with red lenses, crafted from Engineer's Goggles, an Electron Tube and two Red Stained Glass Panes. They work like the regular goggles, and also paint every ship nearby with a colour map of the water pressure on its hull. Sneak and right-click, holding them or wearing them with an empty hand, to cycle through three modes:
  - **Absolute:** red means the block is at the limit of what it can hold and is about to crack. At the surface, the ship is shown as it would be at the depth where it starts cracking.
  - **Distribution:** red marks the most exposed blocks of the ship, whatever the depth, to see how the load moves when the ship tilts.
  - **Off:** no colours, the goggles work like the regular ones.
- **Liquid Density:** A denser liquid presses harder. The new `liquidDensities` server option sets the density of each liquid compared to water; lava counts as 3.0 by default, so a hull cracks three times sooner in lava than in water at the same depth. Depth is now measured in any liquid, not only in water.
- **Burning Hulls:** Flammable hull blocks (wood, wool and the like) touching lava catch fire (`lavaBurnsHull`). Fire on a ship goes out as soon as it ends up under water.
- **Custom Alarm Sounds:** Right-click an Industrial Alarm to open its settings.
  - **Sound:** drop `.ogg` files in `config/create_submarine/alarms` and pick one from the list, the same way the Schematic Table lists schematics, or keep the default alarm. The folder button opens it. On a server the sounds live in the server's folder: the server keeps the files and sends them to the players the first time they hear them, so nobody has to install anything. Files are limited to 8 MB; use mono files so the sound comes from the alarm's position.
  - **Sounding and Pause:** how long the alarm sounds and lights up each time, and how long it stays silent in between, from 0.05 to 30 seconds. Set Sounding to the length of your sound and the light flashes in time with it. A new cycle cuts the previous sound, so they never pile up.
  - **Volume:** from 10% to 300%; a louder alarm is heard further away.
  - **Faster in danger:** on by default, the alarm speeds up the deeper the hull is past its limit, like before. Turned off, it keeps the exact rhythm you set.
- **Repair Command:** Operators can run `/submarine repair` while standing in or on a submarine to fix it on the spot: every hull block the pressure tore out (beams that blew out included) is put back, every crack is sealed, the water let in by progressive flooding is drained, and broken beams stop weakening the hull around them.
- **Deep Seas Config Button:** The pause menu and the title screen get a Pressure Goggles button under Create's, which opens the Create Deep Seas config straight away.
- **Oxygen From Other Mods:** The Oxygen Diffuser now takes any fluid in the `#c:oxygen` tag, so oxygen from other mods can be used.
- **Anchor Recipe:** The Anchor can now be crafted from four Iron Sheets, a Chain, an Iron Block and an Iron Ingot.
- **Seaglide Flight:** Holding Left-Click with a Seaglide while gliding with an elytra now pulls you through the air, at a quarter of the speed of a firework rocket, as long as you have food. It drains hunger twice as fast as swimming. Its speed in the air is set by the new `seaglideFlightSpeed` server option (0.425 blocks per tick, about 8.5 blocks per second, by default).
- **Music Disc:** A new music disc, KANDELWICK - Hidden Between Fluent Waters. It is very rare loot (1.5% per chest) in buried treasure, shipwrecks, ocean ruins, temples, mineshafts, dungeons, strongholds, mansions, ancient cities, bastions, Nether fortresses, End cities and other structures, villages excepted. It can also be crafted from a Nautilus Shell surrounded by four Prismarine Shards and four Prismarine Crystals. While it plays in a Jukebox, on land or aboard a ship, every player within 8 blocks gets Water Breathing.
- **Fore-and-Aft and Triangular Sails:** Sails now behave differently depending on how they sit on the ship. Square sails, set across the deck, are best with the wind behind and cannot beat upwind. Sails set along the deck work like lateen and jib sails: best with the wind from the side, still good on a broad reach, and able to sail up to about 40 degrees from the wind. A sail turned on a Swivel Bearing blends the two. Build the edge of a sail as a 45 degree staircase and the last block of each step cuts itself diagonally, so the sail becomes a clean triangle with a straight edge instead of steps; the canvas billows inside the triangle and stays flat along the cut edge.
- **Halyards:** Sails are now raised and lowered with a rope and a Rope Winch.
  - **Rigging:** with a Rope or a Steel Cable in hand, right-click any block of the sail, then right-click a Rope Winch on the ship (in either order). The sail stays on the ship. The line finds the edge by itself: the full-height edge on a triangle (the mast side), or the edge closest to the winch on a square sail. It is tied at the top of that edge and runs down to the winch.
  - **Growing sails:** add or remove sail blocks while it is rigged and the halyard follows within a second; the rope along the edge and its tie point match the new size of the sail.
  - **Reefing:** reel the winch in and the head of the sail slides down the edge, taking in exactly the rope the winch pulls, so the sail drops onto its foot little by little and pushes less and less. The head slides so the line stays exactly as long as the distance to the winch, even when the winch sits off to the side, so the winch never strains and the sail stays where you left it. Let the winch out and the sail climbs back up. Past 90% lowered, the sail stops catching anything and lets you walk through.
  - **Always taut:** the sail takes up any extra rope, so letting the winch out further than the sail is tall never leaves slack, and reeling in past the bottom stops at the bottom.
  - **Two halyards:** rig both edges of a sail, each to its own winch, and they work together. Whichever winch turns raises or lowers the sail, and the other line follows by itself, so both edges always stay at the same height.
- **Sail and Rudder Ponders:** The Sail and the Rudder each have a Ponder scene, found in the new High Seas category: how a sail catches the wind, which way it pushes, how to rig a halyard on it and dye it, and where to place a rudder, which way to face its blade, how to disguise it and how to steer with it.

### Changes
- **Lighter First Launch:** The welcome screen is gone, so the first launch only shows the photosensitivity warning. That screen now has a More settings button that opens the Deep Seas settings directly, instead of the full list of block strengths nobody needs on day one. Fewer prompts in big modpacks.
- **Lithostitched No Longer Bundled:** Lithostitched is no longer shipped inside the mod jar. It is only needed for the deeper oceans option: install it yourself to turn that option on. Without it, the option is switched off on startup and a screen links to the download.
- **Sodium Is Now Required:** Create Deep Seas needs Sodium on the client (any version). Dedicated servers do not need it.
- **Ship Sails:** The High Seas sail is renamed Ship Sail (Voile de bateau in French), in every colour, so it no longer reads like Create's windmill sail.
- **Sails Work Out of the Water:** A ship needed to float in water before its sails would catch the wind. Sails now work on any ship, including airships and ships resting on land; only a sail that is itself under the water still catches nothing.
- **Sails Push Along the Keel:** Sails used to push the ship along their own facing, so a sail set along the deck pushed it sideways. On the water, the push now follows the keel, from the Rudder towards the bow (or the longest side of the hull when there is no Rudder), and the sideways part is taken by the keel. Out of the water there is no keel, so sails simply carry the ship with the wind. `sailUpwindEfficiency` now sets what a ship keeps on a heading none of its sails can use, and defaults to 0.1 instead of 0.6.
- **Water Culling for Every Liquid:** The pixel-perfect culling that keeps the sea out of sealed hulls now works in any liquid, lava included.
- **Pump Controllers Keep Up With Big Ballasts:** A Pump Controller moved water like a plain Create pump, about 2.5 buckets a second at 256 RPM, whatever the size of the ship. On a big submarine (120 Ballast Tanks for 3 or 4 pumps) filling or draining the ballast took minutes, so the Onboard Computer seemed stuck. When an Onboard Computer runs them, Pump Controllers now push harder on big ballasts: each one is sized for about 10 tanks, and past that its pressure is multiplied by tanks / (pumps × 10), so any submarine swings its ballast in roughly the same time as a small one (around 16 seconds from half to empty at 256 RPM). Only the water flow is boosted: the cog still turns at the shaft's speed and the stress cost does not change. Small submarines are not affected.
- **Credits in the Mods Screen:** The page of Create Deep Seas, Create Abyss and Create High Seas in the Mods screen now lists the developers, contributors, artists, music authors and alpha testers.

### Bug Fixes
- **Crash When Loading Terrain on Older GPUs (#89):** The billowing sail is drawn with a tessellation shader, which needs OpenGL 4.0. On GPUs that only offer OpenGL 3.3 (older Intel integrated graphics, some Mesa drivers), Veil skips that shader, but its render layer was still added to the terrain layers, so the game crashed with a missing shader the moment the world started rendering. When tessellation is not supported, the sail layer is now left out and sails are drawn flat, the same way they are under an Iris shader pack.
- **Client Freezing on Join With Large Ships, FPS Drop Near Sealed Hulls (#91, #86):** The set of cells used by the block-by-block water culling was copied with `Set.copyOf`, which probes on the raw hash code. Block positions of a large, compact hull give runs of consecutive or equal hash codes, so the copy packed them into one huge cluster and became quadratic: on a big hull a single copy took from seconds to minutes and locked the render thread while joining. Every lookup in that set was also about a thousand times slower than it should be, and with Sodium the chunk builder looks up every block near a sealed ship, so rebuilding the chunks around an oxygenated submarine or a boat with a dry hold dragged the frame rate down (flooding the hull emptied the set, which is why breaking a wall made the lag go away). It is now a plain `HashSet`, like the other culling sets, and so are the progressive flooding cell sets.
- **Rafts Sailing Backwards:** A boat without a Rudder whose only sail sits on a Swivel Bearing could start sailing backwards and sideways on its own. The direction now comes from the way that sail faces, away from its mast or yard, and follows it as the bearing turns; the wind only sets the speed. The heading is also kept instead of flipping whenever the boat drifts back a little.
- **Water Showing Through Partial Blocks in a Hull:** The water culled inside a dry hull is a volume of whole cubes, and partial blocks (steering wheels, stairs, slabs, custom shapes) were often left out of it. Each one punched a hole, so the sea surface showed through its hitbox, and everything behind it showed too. Partial blocks walled in by the ship on all four sides and underneath are now always part of the volume. Blocks sticking out more than two blocks above the water (masts, sails, deck railings) are left out, since they hide no water and made the inside of the boat show its water from far away at some angles.
- **Lag With Large Boats:** Three costs that only boats paid, and that grew with the size of the hold, are gone. The leak check walked every air cell of the hold, six neighbours each with a world water lookup, every tick on both client and server; it now only tests the openings of the hold and the top of each column, worked out once per scan. The periodic rescan every 10 seconds rebuilt the cull volume and every compartment set even when nothing had changed; an identical result is now kept as is. Boat buoyancy rotated every hold cell by the full pose on every physics step; the height of each cell is now a three-term sum.
- **Water Patches in a Boat With a Broken Rim:** Breaking part of the top layer of a hull, even above the water, took every air cell lined up with the gap out of the dry hold, cutting a groove into the culled volume. The water is only hidden along the first unbroken stretch of that volume, so looking across the groove showed the sea at the bottom of the hold depending on where you stood. Each hold is now filled column by column up to its highest point, gaps in the rim included, so the volume has no dents.
- **Frame Time Near Sealed Ships:** Every block lookup in the world checked whether it fell inside a dry pocket, even when the block was air and there was nothing to hide, and anything within a wide cube around a ship (seabed and shore included) went through a full pose transform for each pocket. Air now returns at once, each pocket keeps a tight box around its cells and a ready-made transform that is only rebuilt when the ship actually moves, and a lookup only touches the pocket's cells when it lands inside that box. The onboard computer screen also stops re-translating its fixed labels every frame.
- **Blocks Dropping Nothing in Survival:** The Boat Engine and the Anchor need the right tool to drop, but no tool counted as the right one, so they dropped nothing when broken; the Anchor had no loot table either. Both, the Buoy and the Decompression Chamber now mine faster with a pickaxe and drop themselves. The Decompression Chamber also had no recipe: it is crafted from four Copper Sheets, four Glass and a Mechanical Pump. The Oar of Boat had none either: it is crafted from a Plank and two Sticks laid in a diagonal.
- **Implosion Camera Shake:** The implosion only checked the cell under the player's feet to tell whether they were aboard, so on a tilted submarine it sometimes missed them, and when it did kill them the screen went black at once, before the shake could be seen. The whole body (feet, waist and eyes) is now checked, and the screen holds for a moment on the shake before going black. The shake during the build-up is also gentler in its last second.
- **Server Lag From Large Ships, With or Without Sails (#98):** Every 20 ticks, each ship had every block of its bounding box read twice, once to find sails and once to find a Rudder, even on ships with neither. The scan now asks each 16x16x16 section of the ship whether it contains any sail or rudder at all and skips the ones that do not, so a ship without sails costs almost nothing and a ship with sails only reads the sections that hold them.
- **Duplication With the Oxygen Diffuser and the Electrolyzer (#99):** Placing a block above these machines was already blocked, but a Create piston, a Physics Assembler or an older world could still put one there, and wrenching it then duplicated it. A machine with a solid block above it now breaks off and drops as an item, the same way it would if it could not stand there, so existing setups stop working too.
- **Floater FPS Drop (#102):** Floaters were flagged as see-through, so a wall of Floaters drew every face between its blocks. They are solid cubes again and hide the faces they share.
- **Barometer Display Link Overlap (#95):** The Barometer's display mode selector sat on the same row as the Attached Label field in the Display Link screen, so the two overlapped. It now has its own row below, like Create's own sources.
- **Ballast Vents With Pipe Mods (#94):** A Ballast Vent reported itself as a completely full tank of sea water. Create's pumps fill it anyway, but pipe mods that look at the tank first (Pipes N Physics) saw it as full, showed "No Flow: the tank or machine ahead is full" and stopped pushing water out through a vent, including one also used as an intake and the Pump Controller's vents. The vent now reports a half-full tank, so it always has room to take water and water to give.
- **Thick Hulls Showed No Safe Depth and Never Cracked:** Pressure only looked at the layer of blocks touching the inside air, and only when that layer also touched the sea. With a hull two or more blocks thick, the inner layer never touches the sea and the outer layer was never looked at, so the Onboard Computer, the Barometer and the Industrial Alarm showed no safe depth and the hull could never crack. Up to four layers of hull around each compartment now take the pressure on their sea side, and the safe depth shows again. An outer layer that gives way only breaks off; the submarine implodes only when its innermost layer fails.
- **Crash on Startup With Dark Around Me:** Both mods replaced the same call in the light texture update (`@Redirect`), and two replacements of one call cannot coexist, so the game crashed while loading. Deep Seas now wraps that call instead of replacing it, which leaves room for other mods, and never stops the game from starting if it cannot apply.
- **Boats Capsizing Under Tall Masts:** A boat only got its righting force from the air in its hold, so a narrow hull with a tall, heavy mast had its centre of mass above the point it pivots around and kept leaning until it rolled over, sails or not. Floating boats now swing back upright like they carry a weighted keel. The new `selfRighting` server option (Hull section) sets how hard; 0 brings back the old behaviour.
- **Boat Engine Fuel Duplication:** A Boat Engine dropped its fuel whenever its block was replaced, which also happens when a ship is assembled or disassembled, while the moved engine kept that same fuel and dropped it again once broken. The fuel now only drops when the engine is actually broken.
- **Onboard Computer Screen Clicks Breaking the Block Behind:** The screen leans out past the computer's own block, and a click only counted on it when it was closer than the block the crosshair hit. With a wall behind the computer, the upper part of the screen sat level with that wall, so the click went to the wall and broke it, and on some builds the screen seemed not to react at all. The screen now wins as long as it is within a block of whatever the crosshair hits.
- **Sonar Screen Frame Drops:** Every two seconds the sonar on the Onboard Computer rebuilt the mesh of every block it shows, even with the submarine standing still, which caused regular stutters on slower processors. It now only rebuilds when the sonar has moved to another block, and once every five sweeps to catch blocks placed or broken nearby.
- **Repaired Compartments Staying Flooded:** A compartment that imploded (Decompression Chamber, progressive flooding) was remembered as compromised for good, so once its walls were rebuilt it still let the sea in, showed water and gave no air until the world was reloaded. The mark now clears as soon as the compartment is seen breached, so a repaired compartment seals again.
- **Stacked Sails Billowing Wrong:** Each point of a sail took the shape of the first sail (the largest) whose box reached it, padding included. Sails stacked in parallel layers took the size of the biggest layer, and sails meeting at a right angle bent the wrong way. Each point now follows the sail it actually belongs to.

### Removed
- **Furling Sails by Hand:** Right-clicking a sail with an empty hand no longer furls it. Sails are lowered with a halyard instead, which gives every height in between and keeps a rope in charge of the canvas.
- **Water Wheel Propulsion:** Large Water Wheels on a ship no longer push it through the water or spin from the ship's speed. Other mods already do this, and the two behaviours stacked on top of each other. Water wheels on ships now work like plain Create water wheels.

## [3.3.0]

### Bug Fixes
- **FPS Drop on Fast Sealed Ships:** A ship with a working oxygen system (or any boat with a dry hold) made the client rebuild every chunk section around it each time it moved, so at cruising speed (around 1 m/s and up) the chunks were rebuilt several times a second and the frame rate collapsed. Those rebuilds only matter with Sodium, where they hide world blocks such as kelp caught inside the hull; the sea itself is hidden per pixel. Without Sodium they are now skipped entirely, and with Sodium only the sections that actually hold something besides air and water are rebuilt, so sailing through open water costs nothing.
- **Holes Left in the Sea After Joining (Sodium):** For the first frames after joining a world, before the pixel-perfect water culling is ready, Sodium builds the chunks with the block-by-block fallback, which removes the water blocks under a ship. Once the ship sailed away, those sea-only chunks were never rebuilt and kept a ship-shaped hole showing the sea floor. Every chunk section where the fallback removed water is now remembered and rebuilt as soon as the pixel-perfect culling takes over.
- **Boat Engine Shift Placement:** Placing a Boat Engine while sneaking now flips it, like Create's directional blocks and the Submarine Propeller.

## [3.2.0]

### Changes
- **Progressive Flooding Is Now Optional (off by default):** Progressive flooding placed real water blocks inside ships, and that water leaked through closed doors, trapdoors, slabs, stairs, Create machines and casings, while breaking a hull block that touched the sea left broken water artifacts instead of letting the sea in. It is now an experimental server option, `progressiveFlooding` (in the experimental section, off by default). With it off, a breach works like before 3.0.0: as soon as a hull block below the waterline is gone, the sea fills the whole room at once and its water culling turns off.
  - **Submarines:** a broken hull block no longer leaves a "plug" that keeps the room sealed; the room opens to the sea straight away. Breaches saved by the progressive mode are ignored while it is off.
  - **Boats:** a hold with any opening that sits in the sea (a missing hull block, an open trapdoor, a gap in the floor) counts as flooded: the sea shows inside it and it stops giving lift. Plugging the hole makes it dry again.

### Bug Fixes
- **Sea Showing Around Seats on Low Rafts:** A partial block in a hold (a Create seat, a slab, stairs) was only culled when every open side of it faced into the ship. A seat's open top faces the sky above the railing, so its cell was left out of the water culling, and on a raft low enough for the sea level to cross that cell the sea showed through the hole and cast blue sheets behind the seat. A partial block is now only left out when one of its open sides actually touches the sea.
- **Stutter Inside Oxygenated Submarines:** Every time a sealed ship moved by a tenth of a block, the client rebuilt every chunk section around it so that world blocks caught inside the hull (kelp, seagrass) could be hidden. A submarine holding its depth or drifting gently crosses that threshold all the time, which gave a regular frame-time spike as long as you were aboard. With the pixel-perfect water culling active the sea is already hidden per pixel, so the chunks are now only rebuilt after half a block of movement again; the fine threshold is kept for the block-by-block fallback.
- **Startup Warnings Without Optional Mods:** The compatibility mixins for Copycats+, Sodium, Iris and Distant Horizons were always registered, so without those mods Mixin still tried to load their target classes and printed "Error loading class" warnings at every startup, which looked like a missing dependency (issue #87). A mixin config plugin now drops those mixins before their targets are looked up when the matching mod is not installed.
- **Water Staying After Breaking a Block:** Swapping a partial block for air or another partial block does not change whether the hull is sealed, so the water culling was never rebuilt and the old hole stayed. The culling is now rebuilt on the client whenever a block of the ship is replaced by another block (state changes such as a machine turning on are ignored).

## [3.1.0] - Hotfix

### Bug Fixes
- **Triangle Holes in the Water Around Boats:** A boat's hold was any space boxed in by walls on all four sides, even with no floor under it. A hull without a bottom, a moon pool or any open well in the middle of a ship was treated as a dry hold, and its water culling cut the sea into triangles: the sea floor showed through in some places, the water stayed in others. A cell now only counts as hold when there is a hull block somewhere below it in its column.
- **"Batching sections" Crash With Ballast Tanks:** To pick its connected texture, a Ballast Tank walked its whole layer (up to 512 tanks) to check whether it formed a full rectangle. The chunk builder only hands out the blocks of the section being built plus one chunk around it, so a wide ballast layer, or one sitting near a chunk border, read past that area and crashed the game while building the chunk. The search now stays within 16 blocks of the tank, the area the chunk builder always provides; anything larger is treated as a full layer.
- **World Freezing on Join With a Submarine:** In singleplayer the client and the integrated server share the hull strength cache. When a world with a submarine was opened, the client cleared that cache to apply the synced hull values while the server thread was filling it for the submarine's pressure check, on a plain `HashMap`. Two threads writing the same map can corrupt it and lock the game up for good. The cache and the hull value table are now thread-safe.
- **Onboard Computer FPS Drop:** The side-view blueprint of the ship on the computer screen was redrawn ten times a second. Each redraw renders the whole ship again, with its block entities, the entities on board and the outline pass, which cost up to half the frame rate while the screen was in view. The blueprint is now only redrawn when a block of the ship changes (at most twice a second), and every ten seconds otherwise.
- **Water Cubes Around Waterlogged Blocks:** Seagrass, kelp, waterlogged stairs and any other waterlogged block in a hold or a submarine was cut out of the water culling, so the sea showed through a hole around it as a strangely sliced water cube. Only real water blocks are left out now; waterlogged blocks are culled like the rest of the hull.

## [June 24, 2026] - High Seas Architecture, Sails, Boat Propulsion & Submarine Extensions

### Sail & Wind Engine (Create: High Seas)
- **Wind System & Manager:** Integrated a dynamic wind simulation engine (`WindManager`, `WindConfig`) with spatial wind direction/speed sampling (`SailWindSystem`, `SailWindRegistry`).
- **Sail Detection & Physics:** Implemented dynamic multi-segment sail detection (`SailDetector`), sail wind force distribution (`SailForce`), decaying sail dynamics (`DecayingSail`), and structural boat classification (`BoatClassifier`).
- **Dynamic Furling Sails:** Added full sail furling support (`SailFurlHandler`, `FurlSavedData`, `FurlSyncPayload`, `SailCollisionSystem`), allowing sails to be dynamically unfurled or stowed away with accurate collision boundaries.
- **Wind Vane & Goggles Integration:** Added the Wind Vane block, block entity (`WindVaneBlockEntity`), visual rendering (`WindVaneVisual`, `WindVaneRenderer`), client angle tracking (`WindVaneAngle`), and Create Goggle HUD overlay (`WindVaneGoggle`) to display real-time wind speed and bearing. Replaced the legacy Weather Vane block.
- **Custom Sail Tessellation Shader:** Added custom Pinwheel tessellation and vertex/fragment shaders (`sail.vsh`, `sail.fsh`, `sail.tcsh`, `sail.tesh`) for realistic cloth movement, dynamic wind waving, shader state management (`SailShaderState`), and custom render types (`SailRenderTypes`).
- **Iris & Sodium Pipeline Compatibility:** Implemented Iris shader phase mixins (`IrisCompat`, `IrisSailPhaseMixin`), contraption sail baking (`ContraptionSailBakeMixin`, `ContraptionVisualSailBakeMixin`), and `SailSodiumMaterialMixin` for seamless sail rendering under Iris and Sodium pipelines.
- **Reworked Sail Thrust:** Each sail now pushes along its own facing, on the side the wind actually hits, instead of blindly following the hull's keel. Thrust depends on how squarely the wind hits the canvas and on the point of sail, with a configurable floor so a ship can still claw its way upwind. Square hulls with no rudder take their heading from the sails. Furled sails give no thrust at all.
- **Sail Billow Follows the Wind:** The tessellated canvas now bulges toward the leeward side and flips when the wind swaps sides, and the furl animation plays smoothly in the shader when a sail is stowed or unfurled.
- **Wind Debug Arrow:** The F3+B sail arrow now points backwards when a sail is pulling the ship in reverse.
- **High Seas Creative Section:** All High Seas blocks and items are grouped under a new "High Seas" section of the Simulated creative tab.
- **New Sail Block:** High Seas now has its own Sail (`create_high_seas:white_sail`), a full copy of Simulated's Symmetric Sail: same thin shape, same drag on sub-levels, same bounce, same click-to-extend placement, sixteen colours by right-clicking with a dye, and it still sticks to its neighbours when a ship is assembled. It is the only block that catches the wind, billows, furls and gets the wind-side collision. Simulated's Symmetric Sails are back in their original place in the Simulated tab and behave exactly like they do without High Seas. Recipe: two Windmill Sails and a String give two Sails; a Sail can be turned back into a Windmill Sail. The Rudder recipe now uses the new Sail.
- **Sailing Speed by Hull Size:** Sails no longer just push harder the lighter the boat is, which sent a small raft flying at 12 m/s while a big ship barely made 2.5 m/s. Each ship now aims for a cruising speed: it grows with the square root of the hull's length (a long ship is faster, like a real hull), drops if the ship carries too little canvas for its size, and depends on the point of sail and the wind strength. With the defaults, a fully rigged 4-block raft cruises around 8 m/s and anything from 13 blocks long reaches the 14 m/s cap, a bit over half that straight into the wind. The sails make up for the water drag, including the drag Sable applies natively (measured on the fly from how the ship actually accelerates, so no hidden speed ceiling), and then stop pushing, so a light boat can't be catapulted into a wall, and a big ship still takes longer to get going. Sails spread over several sub-levels of the same ship (a sail on a Swivel Bearing, for example) now count as one rig, and a sail on a Swivel Bearing actually pushes the ship: it used to measure its angle against the hull's frame instead of its own and ended up giving no thrust.
- **Heads-up:** Symmetric Sails already placed on existing boats no longer push the ship or billow. Replace them with the new Sail.

### Hydrodynamic Buoyancy & Hold Water Rendering
- **Hydrodynamic Buoyancy Simulation:** Implemented `BoatBuoyancySystem`, `BoatManager`, and `BuoyFloatSystem` for calculating hydrodynamic floating, displacement, and torque forces on boat hulls, accompanied by debug renderers (`BoatBuoyancyDebugRenderer`, `BoatCullDebugRenderer`).
- **Dry Boat Holds:** Boats now use the same pixel-perfect water occlusion as submarines (`BoatManager` feeding the shared `CompartmentTracker`), which replaces the old block-by-block boat culling (`BoatWaterCulling`, `BoatCullBuffer` and `BoatCompartmentDetector` are gone). Water stays out of open holds that sit below the waterline. Boat water culling (`enableBoatWaterCulling`) is now on by default.
- **Progressive Flooding (boats and submarines):** A breach no longer floods a whole compartment in one go. Water now comes in through the hole as real water blocks inside the ship, one block at a time from the lowest point of the compartment upward. You can see the level rise and swim in it, and it tilts with the ship.
  - **Flow rate:** The deeper the hole sits below the surface, the faster the water pours in. Several holes flood faster than one, and the flow slows down as the water inside rises toward the sea level.
  - **Trapped air:** A compartment that has no opening to the open air keeps an air pocket at the top. The deeper the ship, the more the air is squeezed and the smaller the pocket gets. A hole higher up lets the air bubble out, and then the compartment fills completely.
  - **Draining:** When a flooded ship rises and a hole ends up above the water, the water drains out through it, down to the level of the hole.
  - **Sloshing:** When the ship heels or pitches, the water moves to the new low side of the compartment.
  - **Weight:** Flooded cells stop giving lift to a boat, and every block of water inside a boat or a submarine adds its weight at the spot where the water sits, so a flooded ship lists, settles by the bow or stern, and goes down if too much water gets in. Water inside a ship no longer counts as floating volume for Sable's own buoyancy, so a flooded hull no longer bobs back up.
  - **Submarine breaches:** When a hull block of a sealed submarine compartment is broken, the compartment stays a compartment with a hole in it instead of instantly opening to the sea. The ocean shows through the hole itself, the rest of the room floods progressively, and you can still breathe in the air pocket that remains. Breaking a hull block above the water simply opens the room as before, and repairing the hole seals it again. Breaches are saved with the world and sent to every player.
  - **Cinematic implosions:** When the pressure is about to tear a hull block out, it no longer snaps at once. For three seconds the hull groans louder and louder, bubbles and drips pour from the crack, the camera shakes harder and harder until it is violent, and the lights of the submarine flicker faster and faster. Then the block gives way. Above 120 blocks of depth, that is all that happens: the block bursts with a spray of bubbles and a jolt, and the submarine floods through the hole like any other breach. Deeper than that, the hull implodes. The implosion now sends out a shockwave: anyone inside the crushed part of the ship dies, and anyone nearby takes damage that fades with distance (new Implosion damage type, it goes through armour and shields). If the blast kills you, the screen cuts to black at once, even over the death screen: every sound stops, you hear the implosion, then your ears ring for three seconds while one of four lines about the pressure and the depth is written in white on the black, before it all fades away onto the death screen (skipped with instant respawn); if you survive it, the camera takes a heavy shake instead. A submarine that breaks up now tears into many more pieces, cut every four to six blocks along its length and across its height, with a string of underwater explosions running along the hull for four seconds that shake everyone close by.
  - **Crush depth:** Below 120 blocks under the surface (`implosionDepth` in the server config), a hull breach no longer floods the compartment: the sea crushes it in at once, the same way a decompression chamber opened too deep implodes. A compartment that is already full of water has no air left to crush and simply stays flooded.
  - **Decompression chambers:** A chamber opened to deep water before it was filled still implodes as before.
  - **Open doors and hatches:** An open door, trapdoor or fence gate now counts as an opening in the hull instead of a wall. Opening the outer door of a room underwater lets the sea in through it, closing it seals the room again, and opening a door between two rooms joins them into one.
- **Sunken Airtight Hulls Flood:** A fully sealed boat, or a submarine with no running Oxygen Diffuser or Creative Oxygenator, that ends up underwater no longer stays a dry air bubble. Once the sea has covered the highest air cell of a compartment for two seconds, its water culling turns off, the sea fills it and it stops giving lift. A short plunge is ignored.
- **Big Hulls No Longer Freeze the Game:** A large ship run by the boat system (a sub-level with no hull controller) used to rescan its whole volume in a single tick every time a block was broken, and again every two seconds, which froze the game on big hulls and briefly dropped the water culling. The scan is now spread over several ticks, runs only when the shape of the hull changes (and every ten seconds as a safety net), and the water culling is only rebuilt when the holds actually change. The boat system also only runs for a ship that floats at the surface: a sub-level that sits fully underwater or out of the water (airship, ship on land) is left alone and costs nothing. A boat that plunges under or jumps out for a moment keeps its dry hold for three seconds before being let go.
- **Wrecks Stop Exploding:** Ballast tanks are destroyed with the rest of the life support when a submarine implodes. Before, every wreck fragment that still held a ballast tank registered itself as a new submarine, took pressure again and imploded on its own, chaining explosions long after the ship was gone.
- **No Double Ownership:** A sub-level already run by a submarine controller or oxygen diffuser is left alone by the boat system, so the two no longer write over each other for the same ship.

### Bug Fixes & Refactoring
- **Sublevel Cable & Winch Physics:** Hardened rope winch and steel cable connections across sublevel boundaries (`RopeWinchBlockMixin`, `RopeDataSubLevelMixin`, `RopeTrackingSubLevelMixin`, `SubLevelCableCleanup`, `WinchAnchorSignal`), adding network payloads (`CableStrandRemovePayload`) for synced cable strand removal.
- **Water Occlusion & Fog Optimization:** Overhauled `SubmarineWaterCullBuffer`, `SodiumWaterOcclusionBridge`, and `FlywheelFogUniformsMixin` for improved performance and accurate fog density updates in sublevel air pockets (`SableSubLevelPocketFogMixin`).
- **Rockcutting Wheel Assets:** Updated high-resolution textures and 3D models (`spline_block.obj`, `spline_core.obj`, `spline_static.obj`) for the Rockcutting Wheel block.
- **No More Holes in the Sea:** Water occlusion now only hides cells the hull fully fills. Stairs, slabs and other partial blocks on a hull no longer cut a hole in the surrounding water.
- **Out-of-Water Ships:** A sub-level that isn't near any water, for example one flying or sitting on land, no longer applies water occlusion. Occlusion now refreshes as soon as a ship moves or its hull changes, instead of lagging behind.
- **Fog in Air Pockets:** Inside a sealed hull, fog is no longer switched off completely. It now starts just past the hull walls, so the ocean seen through windows keeps its fog. The pocket fog check also tests the exact cell in the ship's local space, and the fog colour is now uploaded correctly.
- **Sturdier Sodium Shader Injection:** The water-occlusion code is now injected after every preprocessor directive (`#extension`, `#define`, etc.) and at the end of `main()`, and it reads the depth buffers with `texelFetch`. This fixes shader compile errors with other mods and packs that edit Sodium's shaders, and occlusion that broke at non-native resolutions.
- **Steel Cable Fixes:**
  - Breaking a steel cable now shows steel cable particles instead of rope particles.
  - Fixed a rope reused after a steel cable was destroyed or detached still behaving as steel.
  - The cable collider is a bit thicker, so you're less likely to slip through it.
  - Cable pushes now move players with proper collision, so they can no longer be shoved into walls or pushed down into the floor.
- **Goggle Detection:** Goggle overlays now use Create's own check, so every goggle-compatible helmet works, not only items with "goggles" in their name.
- **Floater Performance:** A floater cluster now reads the ship's physics state once per tick and applies one combined impulse, and the water surface lookup is cached for two seconds. This makes large floater arrays much cheaper.
- **Oxygen Diffuser Scans:** Diffusers now rescan their compartment only when the hull actually changes, or every 10 seconds, instead of every second.
- **Air Doesn't Lift Submarines:** Air pumped into a submarine by an Oxygen Diffuser is now only for breathing and no longer floats the hull. Between two hull scans, the boat system could briefly take over a submarine and apply boat buoyancy to its air-filled compartments. The diffuser and hull controller now keep their claim on the ship every tick.
- **Physics Wake-Up:** Ships now actually wake up when a force is applied to them. The old sleep toggle did nothing on current Sable versions.
- **Early Config Crash:** Fixed hull-strength and compartment-scan code reading the server config before it was loaded, e.g. on the client before the server config synced.
- **Boat Classifier Crash:** A broken sub-level chain no longer crashes the sail system.
- **Invisible Cables on Anchors & Buoys:** A steel cable or rope started from an anchor or a buoy was fully simulated but never drawn, because only winches and rope connectors rendered the ropes they owned. Anchors and buoys now draw their own ropes too.
- **Anchor Fling:** Tying a steel cable to an anchor no longer flips or launches it (and the boat). The anchor was being dropped half a block into the hull right after becoming its own sub-level, and its arms reached into the neighbouring blocks. It now keeps the position, rotation and speed it had on the boat, and its collision box stays inside its own block.
- **Buoy Placement:** Buoys also keep their exact position when they turn into a floating sub-level, instead of sinking half a block first.
- **Helm Body Jitter:** Looking around while holding the tiller no longer makes the player's body twitch. The body now stays locked to the engine's heading and only the head turns.
- **Helm Seat in the Hull:** You can no longer take the tiller when there is no room to sit behind the engine, which used to put the player model inside the boat's blocks. A "There is no room to sit at the tiller" message explains why.
- **Faster Boat Engine:** Default engine power raised from 16 to 36 (about 1.5× the top speed), so engine boats now outrun vanilla boats. Existing configs keep their old value.
- **Rowing from a Buoy:** Rowing while sitting in a buoy now plays the rowing arm animation instead of freezing in the buoy pose.
- **Seaglide Size in Third Person:** The Seaglide is now 1.5× bigger in third person to match how it looks in first person.
- **Stuck on Steel Cables:** A steel cable running through a block (a winch, a floor) no longer makes you bob in place when you stand on that block. Cable collision now ignores the parts of the cable buried inside solid blocks, in the world or on a ship.
- **Floater Bobbing & Launching:** Floaters no longer bob endlessly, and a lone floater no longer shoots several blocks out of the water. A floater only weighs 0.1, so its push easily overshot. Each floater's push is now capped to what its share of the ship's mass can take in one tick, so it settles on the surface instead.
- **Pressure Without Water:** A submarine sitting in air, for example in a dry cave under the sea or under a lake, no longer takes water pressure from the water above it. Pressure now only counts an unbroken water column rising from the submarine itself: it stops at the first pocket of air, and the submarine has to actually be in water for any pressure to apply. Solid blocks such as rock or ice in the column don't cut it. The Onboard Computer's depth reading follows the same rule.
- **Submarines Rise Straight:** A submarine going up or down on its ballasts no longer tilts and slides off to the side. Sable's own water buoyancy works block by block, so an uneven hull used to pitch as it rose and then glide sideways like a wing. Submerged submarines now slowly right themselves back to level on pitch and roll, the way a real hull does when its buoyancy sits above its centre of gravity. Turning (yaw) is left completely free.
- **Submarines Follow Their Heading:** Turning a submarine used to spin the hull while it kept sliding in its old direction, because the water barely resisted sideways motion. Submarines with a clear long axis now get keel drag: sideways sliding is damped, so the submarine carves the turn and moves where its bow points. Square hulls, which have no obvious front, are left as before.
- **Steel Cables No Longer Sink You:** Walking into a steel cable while standing on the ground could push players, mobs and armor stands down into the blocks under them, and make them fall through a one-block platform. A cable never pushes a grounded entity downwards any more, and the player's cable collision on the client now respects block collisions instead of teleporting the player.
- **Sodium + Veil Crash:** Fixed a crash on start-up with Sodium when another Veil-based mod (such as Flares) also rewrites Sodium's block shaders. The water-occlusion patch is now inserted into the real `main()` function instead of before the last closing brace of the file, the unused fog patch is gone, and if Sodium still can't compile the patched shader, the game logs a warning and loads the original shader instead of crashing. In that case only the water occlusion inside ships is lost.
- **Oxygen Diffuser / Electrolyzer Dupe:** An Oxygen Diffuser or Electrolyzer can no longer be placed, or pushed by a piston, under a block that would end up inside its tall model, which could be used to duplicate that block and its contents. Trying to place a block on top of one of them also no longer makes the item vanish from your hand until the slot refreshes.
- **Floater & Ballast Performance:** Floaters and Ballast Tanks no longer look up their whole group on every physics step. The group (and which block leads it) is now worked out once, on the server tick, and only again when a floater or tank is placed, loaded or removed, with a safety refresh every 5 seconds. Every tank in a big ballast used to rebuild the full group on its own every 5 ticks; they now share one result.
- **Plain Ropes Carrying Power:** A normal rope could carry electricity like a steel cable. When a steel cable was broken, only the end that owned it forgot it was steel, so a new rope tied to the other end was treated as steel. Both ends are now cleared, a rope made with anything other than a Steel Cable always starts as a plain rope, and a rope is no longer considered steel just because of its far end. Blocks that still hold their own steel cable keep it.
- **Hermetic Detection Stuck:** If the hull scan ran into a part of the ship's plot that had no chunk yet, it threw the whole result away and started over, forever, so the ship could stay "not hermetic" with nothing in the logs. Such a spot now counts as open water, and after three incomplete scans in a row the result is used anyway.
- **`/submarine info` Pressure:** "Under pressure" only said yes once a hull block had already cracked. It now says yes whenever a sealed ship is below the surface, and the command also shows the depth limit of the weakest outer hull block.
- **Wind Vane Alignment on Swivel Sails:** The wind vane's alignment percentage now also works when the vane sits on the hull and the sail turns on a Swivel Bearing. The bearing makes the sail its own sub-level, and the vane only looked for sails on its own sub-level.
- **Rope Winch Goggle Info:** Looking at a Rope Winch with goggles showed nothing but the steel cable lines, because Deep Seas replaced the winch's whole goggle tooltip. Simulated's own lines (speed, stress) are back, and the steel cable network and energy are added below them.
- **Clicking the Onboard Computer Screen:** The screen worked out what the crosshair was pointing at only 20 times a second, from the player's tick position, while the screen itself is drawn every frame where the camera and the ship really are. As soon as the player or the ship moved, the invisible click target drifted away from the button under the crosshair. It is now traced every frame from the camera, exactly along the crosshair.
- **Water Inside Boats With a Slab Floor:** A hull block that is not a full cube (slab, stairs...) only kept water out if it was surrounded by the ship on all six sides, so a slab floor, which has the sea right under it, let water show through its empty top half inside the hold. A partial hull block now keeps water out as long as every side it does not fully cover opens into the ship; slabs facing the sea on their open side still let the outside water show, as before.
- **Distant Horizons Water Fog:** With Distant Horizons installed, looking out of a sealed, glazed submarine underwater showed the whole sea bed crystal clear instead of the water fog. Distant Horizons turns the vanilla fog off whenever the camera is not in a fluid, and inside a dry hull it never is. Distant Horizons now treats a submerged sealed hull as underwater and keeps the water fog.
- **Big Ships Stuck in Place:** A boat's keel direction was taken from the widest side of the whole ship, masts and yards included, so a square-rigged ship whose yards stuck out past the hull was treated as if it sailed sideways and got the full sideways water resistance. The keel is now read from the shape of the hull's holds, and the sail speed uses the real hull length. The sails of a ship also always push the hull itself: they could end up pushing whatever other connected piece was found in the water first, such as an anchor resting on the sea floor. A tall ship no longer stops counting as a boat because its masts make it look mostly out of the water.
- **Too Many Sails:** A ship carrying more sail groups than the shader can hold no longer writes past the end of the list. Extra groups simply don't billow.
- **Lava Bucket in the Boat Engine:** Fueling the engine with a lava bucket now gives the empty bucket back instead of eating it.
- **Menus From Afar:** The Boat Engine fuel menu now closes when you walk away from the engine, like any other container.
- **Oars Without an Oar:** Rowing now needs an Oar in hand on the server too, not only on the client.
- **Sails Pushing Players Into Walls:** A billowing sail pushing a player now moves them with normal collisions, so it can no longer shove them into a block.
- **Sail Collision Memory:** Sail smoothing data is dropped for ships that no longer exist, instead of piling up for the whole session.
- **Dry Hole Left Behind After `/sable remove`:** Removing a ship with an air-filled hull (Oxygen Diffuser, Creative Oxygenator) left an invisible dry bubble in the sea where it used to be, until you rejoined the world. The water now comes back as soon as the ship is gone.
- **Upside-Down Pulleys:** Pulleys can now be placed upside down, with the wheel hanging under the block. Place one against a ceiling or on the upper half of a block's side, like stairs. An upside-down pulley only pairs with another upside-down pulley.
- **Electrolyzer Shaft Alignment:** The alternator shaft sat one pixel lower than Create's shafts, so a shaft plugged into the Electrolyzer looked broken. It is now centred like any other shaft, and the hole in the casing was moved up to match.
- **Electrolyzer Facing:** The Electrolyzer now faces the player when placed and can be turned with a wrench on its top or bottom. The alternator shaft turns with it, so it can take rotation from any horizontal side. Electrolyzers already placed keep their current direction.
- **Purple Screen Near Sonars & Alarms:** With Iris installed, even with shaders turned off, getting close to a Sonar or a sounding Industrial Alarm covered the screen in a black and purple checkerboard. Veil never turns on the extra buffers its coloured lights need when Iris is present, so the lights were drawn with the missing texture instead. The coloured Veil lights are now simply skipped when Iris is installed, and a new client option (`veilLights`) turns them off in any case. Both blocks also give off normal Minecraft light (the Sonar always, the Alarm while it is sounding), so they still light up the room without Veil.
- **Industrial Alarm Flashes For Real:** The alarm's lamp texture stayed lit the whole time it was sounding; only the Veil light blinked, so with Iris installed nothing flashed at all. The lamp now switches between its lit and unlit texture in step with the siren, and its Minecraft light (now full strength, level 15) turns on and off with it.
- **Ballast Vent Ponder Rewritten:** The old scene still showed the vent needing rotation and a lever. The new one explains the sea valve as it works now: one face against the sea, a pump in the line, pulling to fill the ballasts and dive, pushing to empty them and rise, and the Pump Controller taking over with an Onboard Computer. It uses Create's usual snow and white concrete checkerboard floor. Only English and French have the new text for now; other languages show it in English.
- **Photosensitivity Warning:** Right after the welcome screen, a one-time screen (same colours, with a yellow warning sign) warns about the flashing alarm, the implosion flicker and the camera shake. Players who already saw the welcome screen get it once on their next launch. Its checkbox turns on the new client option `photosensitiveMode`, which switches off the Industrial Alarm's coloured Veil light, even without Iris; the alarm keeps its normal Minecraft light. Other Veil lights are not affected. The screen is skipped when `disableStartupScreens` is on.
- **Steady Alarm Light:** New server option `alarmSteadyLight`. When it is on, Industrial Alarms keep their lamp, their Minecraft light and their Veil light on for as long as they sound, instead of flashing, for every player. The alarm lamp's Minecraft light is decided by the server, so a player's own `photosensitiveMode` cannot stop it from blinking; this option is the way to do it on a world or server with a photosensitive player.
- **Wind Vane Pointing Backwards:** The Wind Vane's arrow was turned half a turn and showed where the wind was going to instead of where it came from.
- **Rudder Crash:** A rudder on a ship whose pose was not known yet could crash the physics step.
- **Hull Strain Near the Limit:** Once a sealed submarine goes past 80% of the depth its weakest hull block can stand, everyone aboard or close by feels it: the camera sways slowly, the hull creaks and groans now and then, and once in a while a creak comes with a small jolt. It grows stronger the closer the ship gets to its limit, but stays far gentler than the three-second shake before an implosion.
- **Flooded Boats Fill Only the Inside:** Water only fills the parts of a boat that have a deck or roof over them. The open deck between the railings no longer turns into big flat blocks of water: once the sea reaches it, it simply stops floating the ship and the sea shows through.
- **Flooded Ships Sink to the Bottom:** A ship lost the weight of the water inside it as soon as its deck went under, and again once it had been fully underwater for three seconds, so it stopped sinking halfway down. A compartment that goes under now fills completely, and the ship keeps the weight of the water it took on all the way to the sea floor.
- **Hull Strength Rebalanced for Deeper Seas:** Default depth limits are now worked out per material instead of from one straight formula that gave glass a limit of a single block. Glass holds 20 to 30 m, wood around 40 m, stone 60 to 80 m and iron around 100 m. Ordinary blocks stop at 400 m (`globalMaxDepthCap`, raised from 300), except obsidian (500 m) and bedrock (760 m); going deeper, down to the 700 m of the Abyss below 60 m of sea, takes the mod's own blocks. Pressurized glass now holds 700 m, and the Deep Seas machines were raised to 350-400 m. On the first launch after updating, `submarine_hull.json` is backed up and every value still at its old default is replaced by the new one; values you changed yourself are kept.
- **Pipes on the Decompression Chamber:** Create pipes refused to connect to the chamber's back face. The client never saw a fluid connection there, and the server only offered one once the chamber had already found a sealed room or the sea, so a pipe laid first, or a chamber placed before the room was closed, stayed disconnected for good. The back face now always accepts a pipe, and the chamber decides what to do with the water when it actually flows.
- **Magma Bubbles Inside Submarines:** A submarine sitting over magma blocks no longer drags the players inside it down to the floor. The bubble column underneath now only affects things that are actually in the water.
- **Code Cleanup:** Removed dead code (`WaterInterfaceDepth`, `FloaterTuning`), unused textures and a stray model, useless try/catch blocks around plain math, and some per-tick allocations in the boat buoyancy, boat engine, sail shader and water culling code.
